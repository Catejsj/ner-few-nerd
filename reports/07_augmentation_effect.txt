    On the targeted types: precision -0.099, recall +0.068.
    Augmentation did exactly what it was supposed to do mechanically -
    the model became much more willing to predict the rare types, and
    found more of them. It just guessed wrong more often than it guessed
    right, so F1 fell.

[5] VERDICT
    No overall gain: micro F1 -0.005, macro F1 -0.008.

    And it cost the targeted types most (-0.016) while barely
    touching the rest (-0.003) - the opposite of what it was for.

    The explanation is in [4]. Mention replacement multiplies the
    entity surface forms but leaves the surrounding words unchanged,
    so the model sees the same contexts three times with different
    names in the slot. It learns 'this context frame means art' more
    confidently than the evidence supports, predicts art more often,
    and loses more precision than it gains in recall.

    This is a real result about the method, not a failed experiment:
    for a feature-based model whose signal is mostly context, adding
    entity variety without adding context variety does not help.

    Either way, this is the honest answer to 'did your augmentation
    work', and having measured it is worth more than asserting it.
