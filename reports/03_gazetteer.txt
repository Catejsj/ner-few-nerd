[0] CHOOSING THE ONE PARAMETER, ON VALIDATION
    min_count is how many times a name must appear in training before it
    is trusted. At 1, every one-off name enters the list, including words
    that happen to be song titles ('It', 'Yesterday'), and they then match
    everywhere. Raising it trades recall for precision.

    Test is not touched here. The value is chosen on validation and used
    once on test, which is the only honest order.

[3] CEILING
    55,977 of 96,842 test entity mentions (57.8%) have their
    exact surface form in the gazetteer. The rest are names the training
    data never contained, and no dictionary can find them.
    So 42.2% recall is lost before matching even starts. This is the
    number that explains the result below.

    The model fires 173,523 times and is right 46,189 of those.
    Precision is BELOW recall, which is not what a dictionary is
    supposed to do, and it is the most interesting thing in this
    report. The cause is in [2]: a gazetteer built from real text
    contains ordinary words that happen to be names of songs, films
    and books. Once 'It' is in the list as an art entity it matches
    every occurrence of the pronoun.
    Worst offender: organization with 35,179 false positives against 7,735 correct.
