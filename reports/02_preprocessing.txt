[1] TOKENIZATION
    We did not tokenize. Few-NERD ships pre-tokenized sentences, and the
    labels are aligned to those exact tokens. Re-tokenizing would shift
    every label by an unknown amount.

    Few-NERD's own tokenization follows the Wikipedia dump processing in
    Ding et al. (2021): Penn Treebank style, so clitics are split
    ("People 's Republic of China") and punctuation is its own token.

    This matters for the spaCy approach. spaCy runs its own tokenizer, so
    it can split a token Few-NERD kept whole, and then its predicted spans
    do not line up with the gold spans. scripts/approach_spacy.py handles
    this by feeding spaCy the gold tokens directly with Doc(words=...),
    which disables its tokenizer and forces alignment. The measured
    mismatch if we had not done that is reported in reports/05_spacy.txt.

    training sentences        131,767
    tokens per sentence       mean 24.5, min 1, max 267

[2] NORMALIZATION  (applied)
    Applied per token, without changing how many tokens there are:
      - Unicode NFKC, so a full-width or composed character becomes its
        ordinary form and does not become its own vocabulary entry
      - curly quotes and dashes folded to ASCII
      - stray whitespace inside a token removed
    1,280 tokens changed in the first 20,000 sentences.
      '–' -> '-'   x194
      '’' -> "'"   x189
      '“' -> '"'   x56
      '”' -> '"'   x55
      '‘' -> "'"   x22
      '—' -> '-'   x19

    Case is NOT normalised. Measurement in [3].

[3] LOWERCASING  (deliberately NOT applied)
    86.8% of entity tokens start with a capital letter.
    7.5% of non-entity tokens do.
    Capitalisation is the single strongest surface clue an English NER
    system has. Lowercasing the text would delete it, so we keep case and
    give the models capitalisation as an explicit feature instead.

[4] STOPWORD REMOVAL  (deliberately NOT applied)
    26,375 of 340,180 entities (7.8%) contain at least one
    stopword. Removing stopwords would break every one of them:
      World War I                        x133
      the United States                  x74
      University of California           x54
      The New York Times                 x42
      University of Michigan             x31
      University of Toronto              x30
      University of Oxford               x28
      The Guardian                       x27

    It would also destroy the token-label alignment for the whole
    sentence, because the labels are positional. Stopword removal is a
    document-classification technique; it does not transfer to sequence
    labelling.

[5] STEMMING AND LEMMATIZATION  (not on the sequence, yes as a feature)
    We do not stem or lemmatize the text itself: the output of NER is a
    span of the original sentence, and 'Univers of Californ' is not a span
    of anything.

    We do use the lemma as one CRF feature among many, which is the
    standard way to get the benefit without damaging the text:
      Games        stem -> game       lemma -> game
      Studies      stem -> studi      lemma -> study
      Airlines     stem -> airlin     lemma -> airline
      Brothers     stem -> brother    lemma -> brother
      Islands      stem -> island     lemma -> island
    The lemma helps the model learn that 'Islands' and 'Island' behave the
    same way inside a location name, while the surface form is still there
    for the model to use.

[6] SHAPE FEATURE  (applied, as a feature)
    Every token gets a shape string: capitals become X, lowercase x,
    digits d. This lets the CRF generalise over words it has never seen.
    Most common shapes inside entities:
      Xxxxx        63,919
      Xxxx          9,584
      Xxx           4,904
      xxxx          4,675
      xx            2,661
      XXX           1,899

[7] TRAINING SUBSAMPLE FOR THE CRF
    20,000 sentences drawn at random from the 131,767 training sentences,
    seed 42. Entities in the sample:
      location        14,731
      person          11,466
      organization     9,846
      other            5,226
      product          3,424
      building         2,702
      art              2,197
      event            2,163

    Why subsample: the CRF feature set produces roughly two million
    features over the full training set, which does not fit in memory on
    the machine we are using. The gazetteer and spaCy approaches use the
    full training set, and the learning curve in reports/04_crf.txt shows
    what the CRF gives up.

[8] AUGMENTATION
    Handled separately in scripts/augment.py, because for NER the useful
    method is entity replacement, not the word-level swap and deletion the
    rubric lists. See reports/03_augmentation.txt.
